Transatlantic flotsam in the Moominvalley. Valley Sans is a lively and versatile typeface for contemporary brand communication, publishing, and digital interfaces.

Overview

Valley Sans is a clear, versatile typeface developed for the Moomin Company. The design draws from Tove Jansson’s original lettering, the typographic landscape of her era, and details from life in the Finnish archipelago.

The Moomins, beloved characters created by Tove Jansson, are known for wit, warmth, and charm. Valley Sans translates that spirit into a contemporary type system for brand communication, publishing, and digital interfaces while preserving a distinct connection to the Moomin visual world.

Jansson produced a plethora of lettering in many styles. The sans-serif lettering is always uppercase—sturdy, often with slightly flared terminals—which gave a clear direction for Valley Sans. Lowercase and the broader stylistic system were complemented with compatible historical models: late-1800s American modern gothic typefaces, chosen for their warmth, practicality, and expressive charm. The Finnish archipelago, where Jansson spent most summers, suggested “flotsam”: distant typographic influences arriving by sea and settling into local visual culture—part of the conceptual foundation of Valley Sans.

The result is a typeface rooted in heritage and storytelling, designed for clarity, rhythm, and usability across styles.

Valley Sans is delivered as a variable-weight family from Thin to Black in both roman and italic, for editorial and interface contexts. The family is developed for Latin-script use and intended for contemporary brand and publishing needs in digital and print.

Valley Sans was designed for Moomin Characters by Niklas Ekholm in collaboration with Lari Mörö at Helsinki Type Studio.

Body

Valley Sans sets comfortably at text sizes. Use the variable axis for weight without switching files: pair roman and italic from the same family, tune optical weight for interface density, and keep rhythm even when the palette is restrained. This paragraph is set in the roman variable font; the next uses italic for emphasis and contrast within one typographic system.

Italic shares the same wght range (100–900) and is served as a separate variable font with font-style: italic, so browsers can map weight and style predictably alongside the roman master.

Usage

Copy the pattern below; adjust url() paths to where you host the two WOFF2 files (fonts/webfonts/ in this repo after make build). 

@font-face {
  font-family: "Valley Sans";
  font-style: normal;
  font-weight: 100 900;
  font-display: swap;
  src: url("/fonts/webfonts/ValleySans[wght].woff2") format("woff2");
}

@font-face {
  font-family: "Valley Sans";
  font-style: italic;
  font-weight: 100 900;
  font-display: swap;
  src: url("/fonts/webfonts/ValleySans-Italic[wght].woff2") format("woff2");
}

:root {
  font-family: "Valley Sans", sans-serif;
}

.heavy {
  font-variation-settings: "wght" 700;
}

FAQ

What is a variable font?
One font file can cover a continuous range of styles along registered axes (here, weight). You pick a position on the axis instead of installing many static files.
Which axis does Valley Sans use?
wght from 100 (Thin) through 900 (Black), in both roman and italic, per METADATA.pb.
What script coverage is planned?
Latin for this release; scope may grow as the family evolves—see the repository README.
